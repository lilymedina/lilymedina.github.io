I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the [Identity and Conflict Lab](https://campuspress.yale.edu/yaleicl/) at Yale University and a Research Associate at the  [Center of the Politics of Development](https://cpd.berkeley.edu/). I received my PhD in political science from the University of California, Berkeley. 

In most of my research, I aim to understand what drives people to support and participate in violence and what the consequences of that participation are. I am especially interested in the role of formal and informal institutions and social inequalities in shaping these processes. My book project, *Legal Militias: The Case of Convivir Groups in Colombia*, examines the interplay between laws, civilian support for armed groups, and violence in the context of civil war. Across my broader research agenda, I study other forms of violence, including gender-based violence, support for political violence, and police violence.

My work has been published in *Comparative Political Studies* and is forthcoming in the *Journal of Statistical Software*. My research has been supported by the CEGA Development Economics Challenge, the John L. Simpson ABD Fellowship in International & Area Studies, and the Tinker Field Research Grant.

I am a mixed-methods scholar. I combine structured interviews, archival research, and fieldwork with the application and development of recent advances in political methodology.


I can be contacted via lily.medina@yale.edu.
